# App Roles

Version 1 has one role. Every job in [jobs-to-be-done.md](jobs-to-be-done.md) links to it.

## R-1: Salaried Employee

> A Salaried Employee is an individual in Pakistan whose only income is salary from an employer, who has income tax deducted from each payslip and needs to know what they will keep after tax, whether to plan a budget or to compare a salary offer. They can choose the financial year (FY 2025-26 or FY 2026-27), enter their taxable salary as a monthly or annual amount, and see their annual tax payable, average monthly tax and income after income tax. They can also open a step-by-step breakdown of the calculation, see the year's slab table, and follow links to the official sources and assumptions behind it. They must never be shown a tax figure for input the app cannot calculate correctly (blank, malformed or negative amounts, or income above the supported range where super tax would apply), and their salary must never be sent to a server or stored.

**Who:** an individual in Pakistan whose income is entirely salary from an employer. They have no business, freelance, rental or pension income. Their employer deducts income tax from each payslip.

**Example person (a fictional composite):** Hina, 29, works as a quality-assurance engineer at a Lahore software house.
- Her current taxable salary is Rs 210,000 a month.
- She splits a family budget with her parents and is saving for a car down-payment.
- She has an offer from a Karachi firm at Rs 260,000 a month, starting this financial year.
- The budget announcement in June changed the tax rates, and colleagues are quoting different take-home figures.
- She is comfortable with spreadsheets but not with tax law. She has never read the Income Tax Ordinance and doesn't know which year's rates apply to her.

**Context**
- **Pay:** monthly, and planned monthly.
- **Payslip:** shows tax withheld, plus other deductions such as provident fund, EOBI and loan repayments, all mixed together.
- **Timing:** rates change with each Finance Act, usually from 1 July. People talk in financial years ("2026-27"), while the law uses tax years ("Tax Year 2027").
- **Information sources:** colleagues, news reports and online tools. These often disagree, rarely show their working, and may describe budget *proposals* rather than enacted law.
- **Main decisions:** spending and saving plans, and whether a salary offer or raise is worth taking.

**Needs**
- A trustworthy estimate of yearly and monthly income left after income tax.
- To know which year's rules produced that estimate, and how those rules apply to their income.
- To see enough of the basis — law, assumptions, exclusions — to judge whether to rely on the figure.
- Clarity that other payroll deductions are not included, so they don't over-budget.

**Constraints and boundaries**
- They know their taxable salary, or can find it on a payslip or offer letter. They are not expected to calculate exempt allowances.
- They want an estimate, not a tax return, and not advice on minimising tax.
- Outside this role in version 1:
  - people with non-salary income;
  - pensioners;
  - people claiming tax credits or special exemptions;
  - payroll staff calculating monthly withholding for others;
  - tax advisers preparing returns.
